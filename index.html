THE REAL-WORLD PROBLEM

The Invisible Communication Barrier

Sign language is a rich, natural visual language. But when Deaf signers interact with a hearing world that doesn't understand signs, every conversation becomes an uphill battle.

🏥

Emergency & Medical Triage

In high-stress hospital emergencies, every second matters. A Deaf patient trying to communicate acute pain or allergies cannot wait hours for an on-demand certified interpreter to arrive.

🏬

Everyday Independence

Ordering coffee, buying groceries, or asking for transit directions frequently reduces to typing on a phone screen, writing on paper napkins, or being misunderstood.

💼

Workplace & Career Equity

Spontaneous hallway conversations, quick standup meetings, and cross-team collaborations often exclude sign-language users, limiting professional mobility.

🏛️

Public Services & Transit

Navigating government counters, police desks, train stations, and airport security without real-time two-way translation creates friction, delay, and frustration.

😔

Social Isolation & Fatigue

The constant psychological overhead of having to adapt to non-signers creates communicative exhaustion, social retreat, and unexpressed thoughts.

🛡️

Loss of Privacy

Sharing personal medical symptoms, financial inquiries, or private feelings through third-party intermediaries strips individuals of their autonomy and privacy.

📷

Why Camera Apps Fail in the Wild

Optical sign apps require holding a phone in front of one's face with both hands, demanding bright lighting, static backgrounds, and zero hand occlusion — failing in real life.

THE INNOVATION

The Forward Directional Sign Language Concept

Most assistive tools force Deaf users to adapt to hearing interfaces. Our system works in the forward direction: the signer gestures naturally, and the system speaks outward to the world.

From Signs to Speech. From Barriers to Connection.

Traditional communication aids require the hearing recipient to stare at a smartphone screen, download specialized apps, or read text captions.

The AI-Powered Forward Directional Sign Language System (AI-FSLS) flips this paradigm. By embedding lightweight resistive flex sensors and an inertial measurement unit (IMU) into an unobtrusive wearable glove, the system captures finger articulation and 3D hand trajectories directly.

A localized AI classification engine maps sensor telemetry into discrete sign glosses, an NLP layer synthesizes natural, grammatically correct spoken English, and a text-to-speech engine vocalizes the sentence directly to the listener.

01

Signer Gestures Naturally

No phone in hand. No need to stand in front of a laptop camera. Natural spatial signing.

02

Smart Glove Captures Telemetry

5 resistive flex sensors (0-100% bend) + 6-DOF IMU (Pitch, Roll, Yaw) sampled at 50Hz.

03

AI Classifies Gesture Patterns

Spatial-temporal inference extracts joint angular velocities and matches sign signatures.

04

NLP Synthesizes Natural Sentences

Transforms raw sign sequences into polite, grammatically fluent conversational English.

05

Text & Acoustic Speech Output

Vocalizes the message aloud and displays high-visibility text for immediate understanding.

LOOKING FORWARD

Milestone Engineering Roadmap

An honest, phased trajectory from conceptual web proof-of-concept to production wearable hardware.

01
Phase 1 • Current Status

Web Proof-of-Concept & Pipeline Simulation

50Hz telemetry simulation, 14-sign recognition lab, NLP sentence transformation rules, Web Speech API integration, and full-screen pitch presentation deck.

02
Phase 2 • Next Milestone

Custom Flexible PCB & Physical Glove Fabrication

Integration of flexible conductive sensor arrays, MPU-6050 IMU breakout, ESP32-WROOM MCU on custom wearable PCB, and Web Bluetooth BLE pairing.

03
Phase 3

Multi-Signer Dataset Collection & Annotation

Gathering 10,000+ gesture samples across 20+ Deaf signers with varying hand sizes, speeds, and signing cadences to establish robust benchmark datasets.

04
Phase 4

Edge AI Deployment & TinyML Quantization

Quantizing PyTorch LSTM and 1D-CNN models via TensorFlow Lite Micro to run directly on low-power edge microcontrollers with sub-100ms inference.

05
Phase 5

LLM-Powered Contextual NLP Layer

Integration of lightweight on-device LLMs to dynamically translate multi-turn sign gloss sequences into rich, context-aware, polite spoken sentences.

06
Phase 6

Dual-Glove Synchronized Coordination

Synchronized dual-glove telemetry over BLE mesh networking to support two-handed sign language gestures and complex spatial classifier predicates.

07
Phase 7

Camera + Glove Multi-Modal Fusion

Lightweight mobile camera tracking for facial expressions and head nods fused with glove finger bending for 100% complete sign comprehension.

08
Phase 8

Multilingual Sign & Speech Support

Expanding from ASL/ISL vocabulary to international sign systems (BSL, Auslan) with multilingual acoustic speech vocalization in 20+ languages.
